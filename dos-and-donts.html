Dos and Don'ts for Freshers

Practical guidance from students who have gone through their first year in FCI, on what helps and what to avoid.

Do these

  • Attend orientation week fully — it covers registration, department introductions and Faculty rules you won't find written elsewhere.
  • Meet your Level Adviser and course representative early, and save their contact details.
  • Back up your assignments and project files in more than one place (cloud storage and a flash drive).
  • Practice coding and lab exercises outside class hours — computing is learned by doing, not just by attending lectures.
  • Join a genuine, registered departmental or Faculty student association for peer support.
  • Ask questions in class or during office hours; lecturers generally respond well to genuine curiosity.
  • Keep your admission and registration documents organised in one folder, physical and digital.
  • Respect lab rules and report faults to the lab technician instead of trying to fix hardware yourself.

Avoid these

  • Do not miss the documentation deadlines — late registration can affect your course allocation for the semester.
  • Do not join cult groups or secret societies; they are illegal on Nigerian campuses and can end your academic career.
  • Do not submit another student's work as your own; plagiarism and examination malpractice carry serious penalties.
  • Do not skip lab practicals — computing courses are heavily practical, and missed sessions are hard to make up.
  • Do not ignore official Faculty and departmental notices in favour of rumours from unofficial group chats.
  • Do not share your login details (portal, email, or lab systems) with anyone else.
  • Do not wait until the last day to print or submit required documents; portals and offices get congested near deadlines.
  • Do not neglect your general studies and mathematics courses — they are often prerequisites for higher-level computing courses.

If you're ever unsure

Ask your Level Adviser, course representative, or the departmental office before acting on information you're not sure about. It's always faster to ask early than to fix a mistake later in the semester.
